The idea

Two compact modules on the LA-GCN backbone. One brings language in safely, the other removes a hand-tuned hyperparameter.

SEM · Semantic Edge Mask

Inject language by multiplying the skeleton graph, not adding to it

Additive language gates are prone to collapse: their scale s drifts to 0.003, and the language signal stops contributing. SEM turns embeddings of the action classes into a joint-to-joint mask that links joints attending to the same actions, and multiplies the graph by (1 + s·M).

It starts as identity, so training begins from the unchanged backbone, and s grows from 0.1 to 0.22–0.29 on every split.

1 of 8semantic-injection designs beats LA-GCN on NW-UCLA joint: ESA-GCN. The other 7 fall below it.

SAS · Stream-Asymmetric Smoothing

Let each stream learn its own label smoothing

Pipelines grid-search the smoothing strength ε for each modality: 0.05 for joint streams and 0.20 for bone streams. SAS makes ε one learnable scalar per stream, applied only in the loss, so it costs nothing at test time.

All 20 trainings start at 0.15 and settle near the grid-searched values: about 0.06–0.07 for joint streams and 0.22–0.24 for bone streams.

4×gap between bone and joint streams, recovered without any search.

Abstract

Skeleton-based action recognition has converged on compact graph convolutional backbones from 1.3 to 2.0 million parameters. Recent work enriches these backbones with pretrained language priors, but the dominant additive fusion gate is prone to gradient collapse, and the label-smoothing strength is typically treated as a per-modality hyperparameter and tuned by grid search. We propose ESA-GCN, a lightweight extension of the LA-GCN backbone with two compact modules. The Semantic Edge Mask (SEM) derives a per-joint, per-frame edge mask from a frozen class-embedding codebook through cross-attention and a class-summed bilinear form and applies it multiplicatively to the channel-wise topology of the CTR-GC operator. The multiplicative-with-identity-offset formulation is functionally inert at initialisation and is immune to gate collapse by construction. The Stream-Asymmetric Smoothing (SAS) module replaces fixed-strength label smoothing with a learnable scalar per stream, allowing each modality to adapt its smoothing strength to its own noise level. Experiments on NTU RGB+D 60 (X-Sub and X-View), NTU RGB+D 120 (X-Sub and X-Set), and NW-UCLA show that ESA-GCN consistently improves the LA-GCN backbone on every (dataset, modality, split) cell, with four-stream ensemble gains of +0.53 to +0.73 percentage points across the five splits. With a six-stream multi-hop ensemble, ESA-GCN reaches 93.65% on NTU RGB+D 60 X-Sub, 98.02% on NTU RGB+D 60 X-View, 90.19% on NTU RGB+D 120 X-Sub, 92.17% on NTU RGB+D 120 X-Set, and 97.63% on NW-UCLA, surpassing the previously published six-ensemble state of the art on three of the five splits and matching it on a fourth, at a comparable parameter budget. ESA-GCN adds only +2.2% to +2.6% parameters and +4.9% FLOPs over LA-GCN.

Keywords: skeleton action recognition, graph convolution, semantic prior, label smoothing.
